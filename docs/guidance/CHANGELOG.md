# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- **The attitude controller's rate filter is 0.05 s** (was 0.2) for the glide, orbital entry and bellyflop, as the ascent already flew. It went in on 2026-11-20 against 0.5 deg/s of rate wobble the fins chased, in the same change as the fin allocator that works from each fin's deflection and rate and prices a degree moved, the likelier fix; KSA's body rates have no noise, and 0.2 s lagged the damping 18 deg at the glide's 1.6 rad/s (#291). Not flown yet.

- **The hand-over from bellyflop takes a standby plan up to 2.5 s old** (the glide's takes 0.5 s; the standby starts again after 3 s). While bellyflop flies the craft, touchdown's standby re-solves, two iterations each from the measured state, are refused now and then on small rate and attitude errors at their first node, 1.1 to 8 times the gate, so its plan is seldom under 0.5 s old: in the 2026-11-24 15:48 Starship flight it converged three times and was never handed the craft. Once it flies, touchdown re-solves from the craft's state with the flying loop's budget. In that flight the RCS crossfeed (KSAStarship) loaded cleanly and the RCS used 3.4 t, about 5 t before, 0.5 t of it above Mach 15.

- **Starship: control at any attitude, a bank the flaps can hold, and touchdown taking over just before its ignition.** (1) **The whole sphere for the attitude controller and the flaps:** the body and surface tables are picked belly first, nose first, engine first and now, last, from the whole-sphere tables where the sweep has them (KsaAeroSweep.TryPickTables, TableSet.Sphere). Twice a Starship banked past the belly-first tables' 45 deg once its RCS ran dry, the controller lost the air's moment and stopped commanding the flaps, and it tumbled (2026-11-24). (2) **Bellyflop's bank held to the flaps' roll trim:** each plan's bank limit is the largest bank, either way, at which the flaps hold the body's rolling moment on 60 % of their reach, from the belly-first tables at the Mach and pitch flown (logged when it moves 5 deg), never under 5 deg; the Starship had banked to 48 deg 10 km up with its flaps at their stops against 480 kN m of roll. (3) **Touchdown takes over shortly before its ignition**, as the glide's hand-over does: THREE-DOF TAKEOVER BEFORE IGNITION (5 s) before the ignition its standby plan has, or THREE-DOF TAKEOVER BEFORE IMPACT (12 s) before bellyflop's ground. Handed the craft at its first plan, 27 s before ignition, touchdown did not hold the Starship belly first through the coast: it fell from 52 to 99 m/s, every re-solve was refused on turn rates it had not planned, and it lit on the old plan's clock at 152 m (harness, 2026-11-24), where bellyflop had it 16 m off the site at 100 m and the standby's plan, flip and burn alike, was sound. The flopper harness test now flies another craft's design with AFC_FLOPPER_VEHICLE and takes content mods as listed (-ContentMods KSAero,KSAStarship). (4) **Touchdown's standby seeded along bellyflop's plan** (BellyflopSeedPath, as the glide's EntrySeedPath): its coast along bellyflop's positions and velocities with bellyflop's nose as the body axis, its burn a straight braking burn from the coast's end. Seeded free, touchdown planned a coast attitude of its own (60 to 106 deg off vertical), every standby re-solve was refused on the attitude at its first node while bellyflop flew the craft, and the standby had no plan when the hand-over came due (harness, 2026-11-24). Not yet flown with the seed path.

- **The flaps take the RCS's steady work.** (1) The surfaces take an axis from the RCS on what holding a fixed 1 deg band needs (inertia x bandwidth^2 x 1 deg), not the RCS's own band: with RCS DEAD BAND at 4 deg the need was four times larger, and the RCS kept a Starship's pitch and yaw all the way to 1.1 kPa. (2) On the axes the RCS still owns, its torque is integrated into the surfaces' trim over 10 s, held to their reach: the RCS had held the air's steady 80-117 kN m of pitch on pulses through the whole entry, 2 t of propellant by Mach 19 (flight, 2026-11-24), the moment the tables miss. The RCS keeps only what changes. Not flown yet.

(3) **RCS DEAD BAND 4 deg** (was 1), for orbital entry and bellyflop, so the flaps do the work: the Starship burned 5.1 t of RCS propellant, 2.3 t above Mach 20 with the flaps saturated 74 % of the time, 2.1 t between Mach 20 and 8, 0.7 t at the take-over.

- **Bellyflop hands the flip and the landing burn to touchdown guidance** (BELLYFLOP TO TOUCHDOWN, on; TOUCHDOWN FROM, 45 s, under HAND-OVERS). From that long before bellyflop's plan reaches the ground, touchdown guidance solves on standby from the measured state, as it does before GLIDE + BURN's ignition: a coast to the ignition it chooses, the flip and the burn, seeded with a burn at 70 % of full thrust less gravity and a coast at the sink rate now. Touchdown guidance keeps its engine-first table for the 20 deg it covers and fills what it fades out of with the sphere, force, moment and fins: before, past 28 deg it had a roll-averaged drag and lift with no moment, so a flip from belly first was planned on next to nothing. Its tilt limit already opens to the craft's tilt at the start. **Bellyflop's attitude command is low-passed** (0.5 s, read that far ahead along the plan, from the angles flown at the take-over): taken straight off each new plan it stepped every second, and the fins stood at 13-32 deg and saturated 27-38 % of the time, against 3-5 deg and 3-6 % in the entry (flight, 2026-11-24). The rest of that is trim: bellyflop's planner has no pitch moment, so it chooses attitudes the flaps can barely hold; not done yet. Offline: the sphere's angles round-trip (`--orbital-entry`), and touchdown's aero and fin checks (`--6dof-aero`, `--6dof-fins`) pass unchanged for a craft without a sphere. Harness: the flopper test now stages the craft's engines at spawn, flies on to contact with touchdown on (AFC_FLOPPER_TOUCHDOWN, AFC_FLOPPER_LAND_MISS_M 50 m) and takes another craft with AFC_FLOPPER_VEHICLE. - **Orbital entry aims at bellyflop's reach, not a point, and bellyflop takes over as soon as it has a plan.** The entry's range at the hand-over was one point, bellyflop's predicted fall, priced as the dynamics; its corridor closes on that point at the hand-over, so when the fall moved 9 km at Mach 6.4 (flight, 2026-11-24) every entry plan after it was refused for bending its dynamics to it. The range is now priced only outside a **reach band**, bellyflop's falls held at three quarters of its pitch limits nose down and nose up from the plan's hand-over state, at half the dynamics' price (RangeBandShare), with a light pull to the 20 deg fall inside it (RangePullShare). Bellyflop **takes over at its first converged plan** from BELLYFLOP FROM MACH (10); HAND-OVER MACH (5) is the latest, where the entry holds its attitude until one comes. Its belly-first tables reach Mach 8 and are read at Mach 8 above it. Harness (flopper from 100 km): entry refusals went from 44 and 36 a flight to none; the site short of the corridor lands 29 m off (was 248), the one in reach 11 m off (was 254), with heating 102 % of a 723 kW/m² limit; bellyflop took over at Mach 5.2 and 6.5. The flopper harness test now accepts a take-over from BELLYFLOP FROM MACH down to Mach 4.

- **Bellyflop takes out crossrange early, and never flies a stale plan through refusals.** (1) The crossrange is priced at every node, not only at the end (CrossWeight, 300 per km of its time average): with the miss priced only at the end and pitch and bank priced by their squares, spreading the correction over the whole descent was cheapest, and the bank was lazy. Offline (`--bellyflop`, as flown), the crossrange left 60 s after the take-over is 2.2 / 0.2 / 1.5 / 6.5 km in the four as-flown cases, where it was 11.6 / 3.5 / 5.2 / 13.5, and the craft passes 12 km 0.1-5 km off the site, landing within 6 m. (2) The approach cone can no longer outprice the dynamics: it was paid at every node under its top and a defect only once, so with 24 nodes outside it one node's position defect that moved the plan's whole tail inside was the cheaper plan, and every solve from 15 km down was refused with a defect in step with its miss (harness, 2026-11-24). Its total is now held to half one node's defect price (ConeShareOfPenalty). (3) Under bellyflop, a refused plan is flown when it lands nearer than the plan flown now would from the same state (both shot from the craft's state; BellyflopPlanner.ShotMiss): the last converged plan, flown on through 30 refusals, turned the craft 70 deg at 13 km, past the site. Harness (flopper from 100 km, the site short of the heating-limited corridor): lands 248 m off, where it landed 1.4 km off; with the site in reach 254 m (was 200). Both flown before the crossrange price.

- **Orbital entry says when its heating limit cannot be held.** It works out the least peak heating the craft can fly from where it is: the equilibrium glide with the plan's most lift up (its least bank, 15 deg), which no plan sinks slower than at any energy, read along the craft's own drag and L/D schedule. It is refreshed at each re-plan, logged when it moves by 5 %, shown as LEAST HEATING under MAX HEATING while the entry flies, and a MAX HEATING under it puts MAX HEATING x CANNOT BE HELD: THIS CRAFT FLIES y kW/m^2 AT LEAST first on the status line. The flopper (ballistic coefficient about 1230 kg/m², L/D 1.06, from the harness's own drag and dynamic pressure) cannot hold the Shuttle's 440 on a 1 m nose; the Shuttle's is about 440 kg/m², a third of the flopper's. Offline (`--orbital-entry`) every plan's peak lies above the bound (now checked): 381, 450 and 656 kW/m² for the Shuttle-like, belly-flop and capsule cases. The limit stays soft and below the range in price, so where the site needs more heating than the limit, the plan still takes the site: the Shuttle-like case could fly 381 but flies 709 under a 440 limit to make its range. Harness (flopper from 100 km at 7.38 km/s, MAX HEATING 723, three quarters of the 964 it peaks at with none; its least is 630): with the site 6630 km away, inside the limited corridor, it flies 738 kW/m² at most (102 % of the limit, held at 64-66 km through Mach 21-20 where it had dipped to 61) and lands 200 m off; with the site at 5830 km, which the coast leaves short of that corridor, the range wins and it flies 924, and bellyflop, taking over 36 km inside its fall, has every solve refused for its defect from 12 km down and lands 1.4 km off. The flopper harness test now checks the flown peak against a set limit (within 5 %) and reports the craft's least.

Bellyflop **solves from Mach 10** (BELLYFLOP FROM MACH) and **takes over at Mach 5** (HAND-OVER MACH, 38 km), and the entry aims its hand-over at a fall that holds 20 deg of pitch rather than flat, inside bellyflop's reach rather than at its short edge. - **Bellyflop** (BELLYFLOP, on by default; docs/guidance/bellyflop-design.md): a belly-first terminal descent after orbital entry. A new 3-DOF planner flies pitch and bank (the airflow's angles on the belly, ±20°, BELLYFLOP PITCH/BANK) and yaw about the airflow (held to the bearing to the site at the take-over, turned only once bank is at its limit) over a free flight time, falling onto the site; RK4 shooting and one Clarabel QP per iteration, as the entry planner. It solves in the background from Mach 4 (BELLYFLOP FROM MACH), every second (BELLYFLOP RE-SOLVE), and takes over at the hand-over, now Mach 3 at 30 km by default, once a plan has converged, flying its attitude through the entry's own attitude path. The entry aims at the ground through it: its range to go at the hand-over is bellyflop's predicted belly-first fall from the plan's own hand-over state. The entry log carries bellyflop's angles, its plan's miss and end, and the fall. Offline (`--bellyflop`): from Mach 3 the belly-first fall covers 24 km and ±20° of pitch moves it 4 km either way; cold solves land on the site in 0.3 s, and closed loops in air 10 % denser and drag 5 % weaker than planned land within 3 m. The hand-over to touchdown guidance for the flip and landing burn is not yet built.
- A **BELLY-FLOP BALANCE** window in the vehicle editor shows where the centre of pressure sits against the centre of mass when the craft flies belly first, so it can be balanced before it flies. For each angle of attack from 30 to 90 deg, at Mach 5 or 1.5, it gives the pitch moment coefficient on the flight sweep's scale and the distance the centre of mass must move, towards the nose or the tail, to trim there with the flaps at rest. The belly is the craft's -Z by default, or any other side. The planned vertical L/D is **smoothed in the optimisation**, with a curvature penalty and a pull towards the previous plan, so re-solves no longer step the reference and the bank does not chatter between its limits. The tracker's gains come from the plan's reference drag rather than the measured drag, with a 1 m/s² floor. From the fifth flight: the plan tracked to the metre but crept to the corridor's high-energy edge, which the heat load favours, and landed long with no drag to spare. The status wraps, the readouts fall to one column in a narrow window, and PARAMS scrolls. - **Orbital entry guidance**, belly first from orbit to a hand-over at about Mach 2 and 25 km. A new planner chooses vertical lift over drag against energy to reach the landing site inside a dynamic-pressure limit (16 kPa), a load limit (2.5 g) and an optional heating limit, with the least heat load. Each path limit becomes an altitude floor at each energy. Only the dynamics are linearised; the rest of each subproblem is a small QP for Clarabel. It is re-solved every 2 s from the craft's state, warm from the plan before. A tracker flies the plan with Shuttle-style drag and sink-rate feedback, turns vertical L/D into bank from the L/D measured off the game's own aero force, and reverses the bank's side on a crossrange deadband, rolling through lift-up. It may overbank to 120 deg for up to 20 s to stop a sink. Its status line says what it is doing. The angle of attack is the pitch-trim one at each Mach, from a new belly-first aero sweep over four candidate belly axes, with 60 deg as the fallback. A new ENTRY TRAJ window, after the Shuttle's displays, shows range to go against speed between the high- and low-energy lines with the bug and its trailers, drag against reference inside the corridor, the readouts and the parameters. It opens on an entry path, takes the craft at the end of a DEORBIT ONLY burn, and holds the entry attitude wings level at the hand-over. Checked offline (`--orbital-entry`, now in CI): three vehicles from L/D 0.3 to 1.0, in air 15 % denser and L/D 8 % lower than planned, reach the hand-over 1-16 km from the site. Once the trim has chosen the belly side, a second sweep builds **belly-first body-axis and control-surface tables**, the same process as the engine-first and nose-first ones (`BodyAeroFrame`, `KsaAeroSweep.TryPickTables`). AFC's attitude controller then reads the air's moment from them, and the fins' and flaps' allocator its surfaces. They cover 40 to 110 deg from nose-first and ±20 deg of sideslip, to Mach 8. Where a Mach row has no exact trim, the schedule flies the angle of **least pitch moment** in the band rather than a fixed angle. The planner plans the range to go **along the ground track**, negative once the site is behind; the crossrange is the deadband's. A status prefix and a game-log line say when the site is **outside the corridor** (HIGH ENERGY: closer than the most drag can stop at; LOW ENERGY: further than the craft can glide). Each run is **logged** under ENTRY LOGGING beside entry guidance's files: `<run>-orbital-entry.csv` every 0.5 s (site and corridor, drag against reference, the tracker's command and why, the attitude flown, the controller's torques and the flaps), and `<run>-orbital-entry-plans.csv` with every plan published. ## [0.14.0] - 2026-11-14

Landing burns steered with the gimbal, landers with no roll torque, and landing legs that come down by themselves.

The plan of such a craft ended at a fixed heading with no roll rate, which the craft cannot reach, so every new plan was refused and the craft left its first plan within seconds. - **INSERT BEFORE APOAPSIS** moves a free insertion to 5 degrees short of apoapsis, and with OPTIMISE INSERTION on searches from 45 to 5 degrees before it instead of past periapsis. The burn always ends still climbing, so the vehicle coasts up to apoapsis and can circularise there. It is off by default: for most vehicles a burn that ends high costs more than one that ends near periapsis.

- A target entered with its apoapsis below its periapsis now swaps the two when the field is left, so periapsis is always the lower.

- Launch to target now waits for whichever plane crossing comes next, ascending or descending, instead of the one picked by hand. The node choice is greyed out while a target is picked and stays yours for an ascent without one.

- **LAUNCH NOW**, beside WARP and STAY AT 1X when an armed launch offers to warp to its window, starts the ascent to the target's chase orbit straight away instead - for a window just missed. It cancels the armed countdown and heads for the nearest crossing; UPFG steers out the difference in plane on the way up, which costs more dV the further from the window the launch is.

- Launch to target now flies a **co-elliptic** chase orbit, with the target's eccentricity and argument of periapsis on a semi-major axis the offset below its own, instead of a circular one. Clear **COPY TARGET ARG. PE** to leave the argument of periapsis free.

- An ascent to a periapsis inside the atmosphere now inserts where the orbit climbs out of it, rather than cutting the engines in the air. An ascent to an orbit entirely inside the atmosphere is refused.

- The target orbit overlay draws periapsis where a fixed argument of periapsis puts it. With it free, periapsis no longer rides along with the vehicle: it sits where the ascent's predicted cutoff and insertion angle put it, and after cutoff on the orbit actually reached.

## [0.4.0]

Boosters fly themselves home. Returnable stages with their own landing sites, and a move to MIT.

- Added **boostback guidance**. EXECUTE on the new Boostback tab flies a separated booster back toward its landing site through a settling burn, flip, boostback burn, and entry attitude, and it keeps flying whether or not you are watching it. RETARGET works there, so the site can be moved mid-flight.

- Boosters hand themselves over at separation: the stage that drops away starts its own boostback with its own landing site while you stay with the upper stage.

- Added **returnable stages** to the Ascent tab. Any stage that separates carrying a command pod is listed with its own **Set target** button and a live return cost that falls as you climb, so you can see when bringing it back becomes affordable.

- Added **Booster reserve dV**, per vehicle. Ascent leaves that much propellant in the booster and stages on it, rather than burning the stage dry and leaving nothing to come home on.

- The Boostback tab draws where the vehicle will actually hit the ground through the atmosphere rather than in vacuum, with time to impact, impact speed, and the miss against your landing site.

- **The project is now MIT licensed.** ECOS was the only copyleft dependency and forced the whole work to be GPLv3. It has been replaced by [Clarabel](https://github.com/oxfordcontrol/Clarabel.rs), which reproduces its landing solutions exactly. Attributions are in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md; releases up to and including v0.3.1 remain GPLv3.

- KSA 2026.9.7.5402 compatibility.

## [0.3.1]

Fixed the ascent attitude jerk, aimed the launch plane ahead of the pad, and caught up with KSA 2026.8.22.

- Fixed the attitude jerking on the way up: the commanded roll came from cross(steering, position), which is degenerate on the pad and snapped 18 degrees at pitch-over. An ascent now holds the roll it lifted off with.

- Steering is flown as the linear-tangent law rather than a once-a-second sample of it, and the turning rate it implies is handed to the flight computer as the target's own rate.

- "LAN from position" and launch-to-target now lead the pad by three minutes, so the plane crossing lands inside the ascent rather than at lift-off.

- Fixed EXECUTE arming a launch that never fired while the panel was folded or on another tab.

- Added a Force roll checkbox and roll angle to Ascent settings.

- Stage list no longer splits one physical stage across several rows, and names the sequence, engine count and G-limited segments.

- KSA 2026.8.22 compatibility: fixed the panel drawing at half width, and guarded the stage model against the drain simulation double-counting an engine.
