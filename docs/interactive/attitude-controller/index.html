Attitude controller

The torque law AFC's own attitude controller flies in the glides, run on a simulated booster. Start it 73° off in roll and 5° off in pointing, and watch each term of the torque demand.

The law

τ = I kd ωc + I kd Δω̃ + I ki ωn² ∫θ dt + ω × Iω − Mair kp = ωn²,  kd = 2ζωn,  in the model body axes: x pitch, y yaw, z roll
P
The proportional part, written as a rate. ωc = (kp/kd) θ is the rate the error would close at, capped at ωmax = 5 deg/s (CaptureRateDegS), pointing and roll each on its own. Unsaturated it is the PD's I kpθ.
D
Damping on the rate error Δω = ωtarget − ω, passed through a first-order lag of Tf (RateFilterS, 0.2 s), so the wobble in the measured rate doesn't jitter the demand. The target holds still here, so ωtarget = 0.
Integral
The error integrated while it is within 3° (IntegralWithinDeg, pointing and roll each on its own), capped at 8 deg s, at ki = 0.1 /s. It learns what the feed-forward misses. It keeps acting in the deadband.
Gyroscopic
ω × Iω cancels the coupling between axes in Euler's equation. It matters here because the booster rolls fast about an axis with 40 times less inertia.
Air feed-forward
The air's moment read from the aero tables at the Mach number, α and β flown, cancelled before any error builds. On this page the tables know a share of the true moment, and the rest is Mmiss.
θ
The attitude error, ½ Σ ei × ti = sin φ n: the rotation onto the target, from the target's axes ti seen in the body. It is split into θroll along z and θpoint across it.
Why the cap is split. With one cap on the whole vector, a large roll error takes the whole capture rate: ω_c = sat(k_p/k_d · θ) one cap: pointing gets ω_max · |θ_point| / |θ| ω_c = sat(k_p/k_d · θ_point) + sat(k_p/k_d · θ_roll) split: each gets up to ω_max At 73° off in roll and 5° off in pointing, |θ| ≈ 0.96 and |θpoint| ≈ 0.07, so one cap leaves the pointing 5 × 0.07 / 0.96 ≈ 0.37 deg/s while the roll takes the other 5.

The standing error. Without the integral, the feedback I kpθ has to hold the part of the air's moment the feed-forward doesn't know, so the error stands at Mmiss / (I ωn²): 0.17 × 40 kN m / (1.14 × 10⁶ kg m² × 1 s⁻²) ≈ 0.34°.

Fly it

A rigid booster under this law, stepped every 0.05 s. The torque it asks for is clamped per axis, in place of the fins and RCS. Changes take effect at the next step while a run is under way. After a run has finished they re-fly the whole 60 s at once. Hover a plot to read any instant.

Measured on the 2026-11-20 glide and in the harness: the 73° roll at the glide's first step, the 0.5 deg/s of wobble on the rate error, the tables missing 17 % of the air's moment (83 % known), and one cap's 0.3 deg/s pointing with an 18 s roll.

Illustrative the booster-sized inertia (1.14 × 10⁶, 1.14 × 10⁶, 2.7 × 10⁴ kg m²), the 40 kN m air moment held fixed in body pitch, the white noise, and the ±400 kN m pitch and yaw, ±50 kN m roll clamp standing in for the fins and RCS mixers.

What to look for

  • Split cap (main): the roll closes at about 5 deg/s and the pointing closes at the same time, inside a few seconds.
  • One cap on the whole vector: the pointing crawls at about 0.3 deg/s while the roll is far off, and the roll takes about 18 s.
  • No integral: the pointing settles on the dashed line, Mmiss/(I ωn²). Turn the integral back on and it learns the missing moment over about 20 s. Drop the known share to 0 % and the integral reaches its 8 deg s cap, so some error stands even with it.
  • Deadband: give it 1° and the feedback stands down near the target, the missing moment tips the craft out, and the feedback kicks back in. That kick is why it is off by default.
  • Rate filter: set Tf to 0 and the D term jitters by about ±15 kN m on the noise alone.
