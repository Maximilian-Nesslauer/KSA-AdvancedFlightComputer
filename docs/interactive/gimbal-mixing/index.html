Gimbal mixing

How AFC turns a body torque in newton metres into a deflection for every engine gimbal, and what happens when the engines sit off the centre line or a gimbal reaches its stop.

The equation

u = BT(BBT + λI)−1 τλ = 10−6 tr(BBT), −1 ≤ uc ≤ 1
τ
The torque the gimbals are asked for, in N m, in the 6-DOF model's body axes: x and y across the thrust axis (pitch and yaw), z along it (roll). It is the gimbals' share only. The engines' rest moment comes on top of it.
B
The 3 × 2N effectiveness matrix. Column c is the torque one unit of command c adds, from rest. Each gimbal has two columns, one per axis.
u
The commands, two per gimbal: u2i turns it about its own x, u2i+1 about its own y. Each is a fraction of the gimbal's range, −1 at one stop and +1 at the other. KSA clamps each axis on its own, so a gimbal's range is a square, not a disc.
λ
A small Tikhonov term, a millionth of the trace of BBT. It keeps an axis with no authority, such as roll on one central engine, from making the inverse blow up. An unreachable torque then gives a small command, not a huge one.
Where B comes from. Gimbal i has its pivot pi at z = −L, its thrust point ℓi one nozzle further back, and thrust Ti along +z at rest. A command turns the thrust and the thrust point together, first about x, then about y. FillColumns differentiates the whole torque by central differences: τᵢ(u) = (pᵢ + R(u)·ℓᵢ − r_cm) × Tᵢ·R(u)·d̂₀ R(u) = R_y(u₂ᵢ₊₁·θmax) · R_x(u₂ᵢ·θmax) B[:,c] = (τᵢ(+h·e_c) − τᵢ(−h·e_c)) / 2h, h = 0.5 For one engine on the centre line this gives τx = −L T sin(uθmax) exactly: the nozzle's swing and the side force cancel, and the arm is the one to the pivot. So the gain per unit command is L T θmax whatever the nozzle's length, less the central difference's slope error, sin(hθ)/(hθ), 0.05 % at 6°. Measured taken at the rest thrust point instead, a booster's torques came out 1.37 times what its engines gave (2026-10-08).

After the solve

The closed-form solve is the start. Three things follow in KsaTvcAllocator.Solve, and the explorer below shows each.

The weak axis gives way

An axis with under a quarter of the strongest axis's capability (WeakAxisShare, usually roll) is clamped to what it can do and allocated last, in the travel pitch and yaw leave. Measured one craft had 2.8 kN m of roll against 1902 kN m of pitch.

Redistribution at the stops

If a command passes 1, the whole solution is scaled to the first gimbal at its stop. Then Redistribute holds that gimbal there and solves the rest on the gimbals still free, pass by pass. Measured without it a landing got 51–69 % of its torque (2026-11-21).

The coupled roll

An engine off the axis rolls the craft as it steers: τz,coupled = (mxτy − myτx) / L, with m the rest moment per newton. Named apart as coupled, it is solved with pitch and yaw, not held at zero by the verniers.

See it

Drag on the torque plot, or focus it and use the arrow keys, to set τ across the thrust axis; the roll slider sets the plan's own roll. Click an engine on the plate to light or unlight it. Everything below is the JavaScript port of KsaTvcAllocator.Solve running live.

Things to try

  1. Gain. On Harness test, read the engine's column under the plate: 839.6 kN m per unit command against L T θmax = 840.0. Move the nozzle slider: the column does not change.
  2. No roll on one central engine. Same preset: the roll slider gives nothing, and the roll capability reads 0. Roll is the weak axis and λ keeps the solve finite.
  3. Trim. On Triple plate, light E1 alone. The rest moment is 0.8 m per newton on a 10 m arm, so the trim is atan(0.08) = 4.57°. Hold the trim puts the demand on it; the linear columns send E1 to 4.59°, the central difference's 0.13 % slope at 10°.
  4. Coupled roll. Light E1 and both verniers and drag along τx. The roll bar's demand moves by (mxτy − myτx) / L. Switch Name the coupled roll off: the verniers must hold the roll at zero and reach their stops, and the dashed curve shrinks to a sliver, which is where E1 stops with Redistribute off too. With redistribution on, E1 carries on, but as a least-squares compromise: the craft gets the roll anyway, unasked, and it shows as a roll shortfall.
  5. Redistribution. On Harness test, drag the demand past the right edge of the square, a little above the axis. Scaled to the first stop (the hollow point), the whole demand shrinks and the τy goes with it. Redistributed, the x axis holds at its stop, the box flashes, and the y axis carries on until τy is met. Across gimbals it is the same: on Triple plate with the coupled roll unnamed, the verniers stop first (badges 1 and 2) and E1 carries on.
