Fin mixing

How AFC turns a torque demand into fin deflections, one linear programme a step, from where each fin is now and only as far as its rate reaches. This page runs a JavaScript port of the allocator on main, with its LP solver.

The equation

τ0 + B u + Σ tk dk + e = τ0 ≤ uj ≤ wj   0 ≤ dk ≤ 1
τ
The torque the attitude controller asks for, in N m, in its own frame. Here that frame is pitch, yaw and roll, with roll third.
τ0
What the fins give where they are now. Every fin's curve is zero at rest.
uj
How far a fin travels along segment j of its curve this step, in degrees. Each fin's travel is split outward each way from where it is now, at its 15° table points, at rest, and at its rate reach, so 0 ≤ uj ≤ wj keeps every fin inside what it reaches by the next step.
B
Column j is segment j's slope bj, its torque per degree. A segment steeper than the one inside it is merged with it, so the slopes only get shallower outward and the programme fills them inside first.
tk dk
The RCS backup columns: thruster k's torque at full duty, times its duty between 0 and 1. The best thruster costs 0.02 per N m. These columns are absent with the backup off.
e
The shortfall, e+ − e−, priced 1 per N m at priority 1 and 0.3 in roll in flight. A fin degree that buys 1 kN m costs about 0.001 per N m, so the fins give all they can before any demand goes unmet.
The programme, and its refinement. Each pass minimises Σⱼ (±c_δ ± c_Δ) uⱼ + Σₖ cₖ dₖ + Σₐ wₐ (e⁺ₐ + e⁻ₐ) c_δ = 10⁻³ × the best segment's slope (a degree away from rest; earned back toward rest) c_Δ = c_δ / 2 (a degree moved; earned back toward where the fin is now) The first pass is the programme on the straightened segments. It is exact where a fin stalls the usual way. Where a curve bends the other way, its answer can count on torque the fin does not give, so Allocate refines it:
  1. Give each fin one straight slope, the secant of its curve across a window of ±W around the best answer so far, the same slope both ways.
  2. Solve, then read what the new deflections really give off the curves and the true cost in the programme's own prices.
  3. If the cost fell, keep the answer and double W, up to 15°. If not, halve it.
  4. Stop when W is under 0.05°, after 24 passes, or once the planned torque is within 1 + 0.005 |τ| N m of what the curves give. W starts at 10°. If the first start does not settle, a second runs from where the fins are now, and the better answer is kept.

See it

Drag anywhere on the plot to set the demand τ, or focus the plot and use the arrow keys. Every change re-runs the allocation from where the fins are now, with each fin's rate reach this step. Step moves the fins to that answer and advances the clock by Δt; Run keeps stepping. The arrows add up each fin's torque, then the RCS backup's, tip to tail.

Things to try

These are the cases AFC's offline check AeroAllocatorCheck tests, on the cross fins.

  • 12 kN m of pitch from rest, nothing else: fins 0 and 2 go to 6° each, half the work each so their rolls cancel.
  • 36 kN m of pitch: into the stall, each pitch fin at 15 + 2 × 3 = 21°.
  • 60 kN m of pitch: out of reach. The fins give 45 kN m at their stops and fall 15 kN m short.
  • Set rate × Δt to 2° (20°/s and 0.10 s) and ask for 36 kN m from rest: one step gets the pitch fins to 2° and 4 kN m, no further.
  • Drag the fins to 8, 0, 4 and 0°, then press What the fins give now. A demand already met moves nothing, though 6 and 6° would be cheaper from rest.
  • Put every fin at 5°, zero the demand and step with a 1° reach: each fin moves 1° toward rest.
  • With the backup on, ask for 45 kN m of pitch and 10 kN m of roll. The pitch pair gives all the pitch, the yaw pair rolls against each other, and the RCS gives the roll left over.

Illustrative the cross fins are the made-up curve of the offline check: 1000 N m a degree to 15° and half that beyond, with a roll of 200 N m a degree. The 20°/s fin rate is assumed, not measured. The RCS backup is six pure-axis columns of 5 kN m at equal propellant flow, so each costs 0.02 per N m; the offline check uses two of them.

Measured the 2stage_KSAero rows are the harness's sweep of that craft's four ±45° fins at Mach 0.3 and 6 kPa, engine first (2026-11-20), as stored in the offline check. Their asmb rows are reordered into the controller's frame: asmb y as pitch, z as yaw and x as roll. Their side forces are not in the check, so none is shown.

The outline is what the fins reach from rest with no rate limit and the off-plane axis held at zero: for every 2° of direction, the LP that maximises t with Bu = t · direction on the straightened segments. On curves that bend the wrong way that LP can promise a little more than the fins give, which is why Characterise checks its answer by allocating it.
