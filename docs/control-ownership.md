# Vehicle control writes

`VehicleControlOwnership` coordinates claims between AFC's RCS executor and Guidance.
It is internal to AFC and provides no shared ownership protocol for other mods.
Conflicting writes from another mod are outside this arbitration.

RCS control and the guidance driver are active in the current build.
Planning features also edit burn plans; this page focuses on control, release and staging.

## Stock burn mode

Guidance and the RCS executor take stock's burn mode through `StockBurnMode`.
`StockBurnMode.HoldManual` selects Manual when the mode was Auto and says so, and `StockBurnMode.GiveBackAuto` gives an Auto back when the controller lets go.
The writes mirror `Vehicle.SetEnum` without going through it, because AFC intercepts that path to start and cancel RCS burns.
So the navball frame follows the mode, the vehicle region's frame for Manual and BurnBody for Auto, and a craft without a control module gets no Auto back, as stock refuses it.
A give-back also needs the mode to still read Manual and the loaded burn target to be the one the caller names.
Stock `FlightComputer.ComputeControl` drops a started Auto burn to Manual and sets `FlightComputer.AutoBurnStoppedOutOfPropellant` when no active engine reports propellant, and `FlightComputer.RaisePendingAlerts` then ends it. `StockBurnMode.GiveBackAuto` therefore leaves such a burn in Manual.
AutoStage is the one exception: while its staging (or one by the player during an Auto burn) is in flight, it clears that flag and `BurnTarget.LastIgnitionDenied` in the `Universe.ApplyVehicleSolvers` postfix, before stock reads them, and a `Vehicle.PrepareWorker` prefix reports the new engines as fed for their first worker tick.
MultiPass re-engages Auto for its next pass through `Vehicle.SetEnum`, so that request reaches the RCS interception.
After an ascent with THEN set to a moon on the LAUNCH page, guidance adds each burn of the chain to that moon (the injection, at most two corrections, and the capture when it was asked for) with `FlightComputer.AddBurn` and selects Auto through `Vehicle.SetEnum` in the same way (`GuidanceWindow.ArmMoonBurn`). It does this only after the ascent release has given the claim back, only into an empty burn plan with Auto not selected, and only while no controller holds the craft. It holds no claim for a burn: stock flies it, and the node and the burn mode are the player's. It does not make the Auto request while the burn resolves to the RCS executor (`RcsExecutor.WouldExecuteRcs`). A node the player removes or changes, another planned burn, or Auto off for more than a staging with AutoStage takes stops the chain, which then leaves the node, the burn mode and the engine as they are. The pick of Stay in orbit, another controller on the craft, guidance switched off or a fault in a chain step stops it too, and then the chain selects Manual through `StockBurnMode.HoldManual` on its own loaded burn while that burn has not started (`GuidanceWindow.DisarmMoonBurn`), so the node stays planned and does not ignite by itself. When guidance has taken the craft and holds that burn in Manual, the chain drops the give-back of its Auto instead.

## Burn plan removal

Stock `FlightComputer.EndAutoBurn` removes a finished or stopped Auto burn when another burn follows it. AFC acts only after that, in a postfix on `FlightComputer.RaisePendingAlerts`: MultiPass removes its own pass and AutoRemove the last finished burn, each only while it is still in the plan.
The chain to a moon removes its own node after stock has completed the burn, when it is the only burn left in the plan (`GuidanceWindow.MoonBurnComplete`), because it plans its next burn only into an empty plan.
The follow-up of a flyby departure does the same with the departure or correction it waited for, at its course check (`FlybyCourse.CheckCourse`), and only while Auto is not selected.
An RCS burn takes the same path: the executor raises `FlightComputer.AutoBurnCompleted` or `AutoBurnStoppedOutOfPropellant` itself in the `Universe.ApplyVehicleSolvers` postfix, so stock ends it like an engine burn.

## Yielding the attitude

AFC takes the attitude once, at acquisition, and never takes it again for the same execution.
Rate hold and Align record a command for comparison on later steps.
The comparison checks the fields the live tracker reads for the recorded target.
Mode and tracker always count. The frame and the coordinates count under None and under Custom, because `FlightComputer.UpdateAttitudeTarget` reads the coordinates as a rotation rate under None and as an orientation under Custom, and reads the frame in both.
They do not count for a built-in target, where that method derives the frame itself and a change is the game rather than a takeover.
A value that no longer matches means a player click or another mod points the craft, so AFC yields, drops Align to Hold, stops requiring an attitude for its firing gate, stops claiming a mode to hand back, and leaves the new tracker alone at release.
Selecting another tracked target leaves the mode at Auto, so checking the mode alone is insufficient.
The yield is recorded in `attitude_yielded` and survives a save, so a load cannot take the attitude back from the player.
A burn that finds RCS disabled after acquisition cancels with a visible message and leaves RCS disabled.
The executor detects changes on its next step.
Cleanup also checks for a takeover before restoring attitude, because cancellation can bypass the step check.
A save does not carry the recorded command, so an execution that has not yielded writes its align target again after a load.
A hold burn has no such rewrite, so reconciliation adopts the loaded values as its own command, but only when it has nothing recorded, it still holds the attitude, and the loaded mode is the Auto its rate hold leaves behind.
Reconciliation rebuilds control state from all ownership markers to prevent another acquisition after loading.
`afc-rcs-attitude-yield` checks target changes, a rate and a frame change under None, a changed euler target under Custom, Manual selections, release, and simulated load recovery.
The tests call `Vehicle.SetEnum` directly rather than queue UI input, and load cases reset transient state rather than save and load the game.

Selecting None does not by itself establish a neutral rotation command.
`FlightComputer.UpdateAttitudeTarget` reads `CustomAttitudeTarget` as a rotation rate while tracking is None, and both `RateHold` at acquisition and the release select None.
Coordinates left there are a standing turn whoever wrote them, and the tracker they pointed with is gone once AFC took the attitude, so the release clears them.
`afc-control-write-surface` sets a custom target, takes control through the executor, cancels, and measures the commanded rate after a game step.

## Guidance stopping on a player takeover

Guidance writes one attitude command, Custom in a chosen frame, and `AttitudeOwnership` compares that whole command on every step.
A mismatch means a player or another mod steers now, so the modes that need that attitude stop.
Stopping is not a shutdown request. The release hands back the rate and the gimbal override as it always does, keeps the player's attitude fields, leaves the engine command where the flight left it, and says why guidance stopped.
A takeover moves the attitude and nothing else, so an engine cut guidance already decided on still happens.
That covers the queued one-shot cut, which touchdown, a failed landing solve, an engine-wait failure, a boostback abort and a 6-DOF disengage all set, and it covers a release that shuts down by itself, which the ascent release records in `ShutdownRequested`.
A landing abort queues that cut from every other phase, and hands the craft back with the engine as it is from the powered descent and the terminal hover, because a cut in the air drops the craft. `afc-guidance-landing-abort` covers both.
Only a takeover with neither of those pending leaves the engine command alone.
The release makes that call, not the takeover, so a cut decided after the takeover still happens. That matters when a cleanup failed, because the no-cut request waits for the retry and an abort can arrive between the two.

A step that hands the craft to another guidance mode is not a stop either. The 6-DOF dispatch releases only when the step left no mode running, because the handover to terminal hover claims the craft again, and a release in the same step would cut the engine the handover kept lit and reset the mode that just started.
The deorbit burn's handover to 6-DOF is the same rule in the other direction. From its handover time the burn keeps flying the craft while touchdown guidance solves on standby, which writes nothing to the craft, and hands over only once the standby has a fresh plan. It then queues an engage that the next step's dispatch runs, so the release after mode processing keeps a craft that has a 6-DOF engage queued, and the engine holds the burn's command until the engage runs. A standby with no plan in the last seconds of the burn gives the craft to G-FOLD when G-FOLD can fly it. The handover to G-FOLD writes the descent's engine command on the step it switches, before G-FOLD has a plan, so the burn's throttle carries over until the first plan replaces it. `afc-guidance-landing-handoff` steps these handovers.
Touchdown guidance does not end in the air with an engine cut. An engage that cannot configure the 6-DOF problem for the craft (rare: an engine thrust point on the centre of mass, a degenerate inertia, or a minimum throttle that needs more tilt than MAX TILT allows when MAX TILT is under 90 deg), a cold solve that gives up or has no plan after 6 s, and a solver fault start a G-FOLD descent when G-FOLD can fly the craft: an airless body, liquid engines that throttle, and a gimbal. The step sets the landing phase, so the dispatch reads it as a handover and does not release the craft, and the descent starts from the engine as it is, lit at its throttle or off. Where G-FOLD cannot fly the craft, a cold solve that gives up and a fault keep the craft with touchdown guidance, which solves again and writes the engine command of its no-plan law meanwhile, off until the stop on the pad needs thrust. A refused engage of a lit burn, and a second fault within a second, release the craft with the engine as it is and say so. After the stop-short cut the engine stays off only while the fall ends at the pad at 3 m/s or less, and from higher up the no-plan law writes the engine command. DEORBIT + LAND refuses a craft that neither solver can fly before the deorbit burn. `afc-guidance-touchdown-fallback` steps the refused engage, the fault and the refusal.
The no-cut request and the reason both survive a failed cleanup, so a retry can neither deliver a late shutdown nor lose why guidance stopped.
Holding the last throttle is not a claim that the trajectory is safe, and continuing an automatic throttle under player steering would be a separate mode capability with its own tests.
`afc-guidance-player-takeover` covers the stop, the queued cut, and the failed cleanup with its retry.

## Guidance control

`GuidanceFeature` installs a prefix on `Vehicle.PrepareWorker` that runs `GuidanceWindow.ApplyAutopilot` for every vehicle, after `InputEvents.ApplyInputEvents` has drained the player's input and before the worker snapshot, so an engine command written there is the one the worker sees.
The same block installs the rate postfix on `FlightComputer.UpdateAttitudeTarget`, and `VehicleCommandSink.Run` dispatches the gimbal writer after the RCS writer.
A step that throws keeps the craft on its last attitude target and engine command, because an engine cut in the air costs more than one bad step.
A failed step adds two to a count and a good step takes one away, so a steady fault releases the craft through `GuidanceWindow.FailAutopilot` after `GuidanceWindow.MaxFailedSteps` steps, and faults on more than a third of the steps release it in the end.
A craft with an engine cut queued, or a held craft on the ground outside an ascent, is released at the first failed step. An ascent keeps the window at lift-off. Each kind of fault is reported once.
The readouts, which are the return cost and the trace, run in their own guard. A fault there is reported once, waits a second before the next try, and does not count as a failed step. The stage-model refresh catches its own faults, and the booster hand-over and the launch window start modes, so their faults count as failed steps like a mode's.
The **GUIDANCE ENABLED** switch of the AFC menu, and the same switch on TUNE > PREFERENCES in the AFC console, queues a release for every held craft, applied on each craft's next step.

`afc-guidance-driver` installs these hooks and steps the universe with the ascent step replaced. It checks the claim, the attitude and engine writes, the rate on the worker, the burn-mode hold with its takeover, replaced-burn and no-cut cases, the sink dispatch, the Enabled switch, a readout fault that leaves the mode flying, a run of failed steps that releases the craft, faults on every other step that release it later, a rare fault that does not, and a queued cut that releases it at once. `afc-guidance-staging-arm` checks the arm-once rule against the real AutoStage switch.

The release paths keep the existing flight computer and burn plan objects.
That does not mean AFC never changes plans, parts or staging through other features and stock APIs.

## Commit and stop in the UI

The AFC console starts and stops modes only through the guidance entry points named below. A draw never writes a `FlightComputer` field or `_manualControlInputs`. The only request the console adds is a stop for a craft that is not the controlled one, which `GuidanceWindow.RequestStop` queues and the craft's next `Vehicle.PrepareWorker` prefix applies through the same abort entry point before its modes step.

Commit. The footer of a page commits the mode of that page: `ExecuteAscent` for LAUNCH, `ExecuteBoostback` for LAND > BOOSTBACK, `ExecuteLanding` for LAND > DEORBIT, `StartGfoldNow` or `Engage6Dof` for LAND > LAND NOW with G-FOLD or TOUCHDOWN, the commit hook of entry guidance for LAND > LAND NOW with ENTRY, and `StartTerminalHover` for the hover. The entry guidance commit sets `AutoStage` and `ThreeDofEngagePending`; the next step of the craft takes the request in `ApplyAutopilot` and calls `Engage3Dof` ahead of the control claim. During a deorbit that stock AUTO flies, the commit first ends that landing with `AbortLanding`, the same entry point as its stop, which cuts the engine, because `StepStockDeorbit` returns before the request is read and the request would otherwise wait for the end of the deorbit. This is the one place where a commit ends another mode itself and not through the claim of the new mode. While the mode of a page runs, that page has no verb, only its status, so the footer never starts a running mode again. A verb that does not fit the situation of the craft (`SituationVerbs`) is an outline with a plain reason, and a press still calls its entry point, so only a refusal in `CommitResolver` or in the mode itself stops it. The click is acted on after `ConsoleStyle.EndFooter`, as stock acts on its own button in `TransferPlanner.DrawPlanWindow`. A commit that would take the craft from another running guidance mode shows its verb as TAKE OVER and asks first in the footer (`TakeOverAsk`), because a stock modal would dim the HUD and block the stop while the craft flies. The question ends by itself when the page, the verb or the running mode changes. The action runs on a later frame than the first press, so it checks again that guidance is still on, that the craft is not disposed and that it is still the controlled one before it binds the guidance state to it and makes the call. `StartGfoldNow` and `StartTerminalHover` turn AFC MAY STEER and AFC MAY STAGE AND THROTTLE on by themselves, and the console says so once.

The maneuver commits keep their paths: CREATE NODE and CREATE n PASSES on MANEUVER go through `MultiPassCommitter.QueueAddBurn` or `MultiPassController.Start`, TRANSFER through `TransferCommitter`, which creates the transfer and the flyby departure with the delta v refined for the finite burn (`TransferFlownPlan`, `HohmannFlybyUI.RefineDeparture`) as stock refines its own CREATE, and FLY BURN sends the same `InputEvents.FlightComputerInputData` as AUTO on the stock BURN gauge.

After stock has completed the single flyby departure that TRANSFER created to a moon of the craft's parent, `FlybyCourse` compares the periapsis on the craft's flight plan with the requested one and, when it is off, adds a correction node with `FlightComputer.AddBurn` (`CourseCorrector.TryAddNode`, the same call the chain to a moon makes), at most two after one departure. It adds a node only into an empty burn plan with Auto not selected, selects no burn mode and holds no claim: the node is loaded in Manual, and FLY BURN or the stock BURN gauge flies it like the departure. A node the player removes or changes, another planned burn at the check, or a claim on the craft at the check ends the follow-up, which then leaves the plan as it is.

Stop. The stop in the console head acts on what runs on the craft, not on the page on screen: a guidance mode first, in the stop order of `GuidanceModes` (a touchdown guidance descent that is not the landing machine, entry guidance, the hover, the powered descent, the landing chain, the boostback, the ascent), then an RCS burn, then a multi-pass, then a node that stock AUTO flies. The same stop is in the strip that stays while the console is closed, and as the first item of the AFC menu. A mode that ends by itself while the craft still needs flying marks a failure (`VehicleAutopilotState.FailureWhat`). That mark changes no control: the console shows the red band under the stock altitude counter until it is acknowledged, and `ClaimVehicle` clears it when a new mode starts. With nothing running there is no stop. The stop is one red button labelled STOP and the name of what runs, and the row under it says what happens to the engine (`StopResolver` is the source of that text; the tooltip has the full sentence):

| STOP STOCK AUTO | `InputEvents.FlightComputerInputData` with `FlightComputerBurnMode.Manual` | Stock AUTO goes back to MANUAL, so its burn ends: `Vehicle.PrepareWorker` holds the manual engine switch off while AUTO flies, and `FlightComputer.ComputeControl` reads that switch in MANUAL. The node stays. |

A guidance mode that registers through `GuidanceWindow.RegisterMode` takes its place in the same stop order with its own stop number, and its stop calls the stop hook it registered, with the label and engine outcome its descriptor names, or the outcome its `StopText` hook gives for what it does now. Entry guidance is such a mode, with stop number 15. A boostback glide hands the craft to it with no commit, so the head and the stop read it from the state of the craft, not from a page. Without a controlled craft (the editor, an EVA, no craft), the head shows the stop of the one other craft AFC still flies and names that craft; with several, their stops are under OTHER CRAFT in the head drawer. A guidance stop for a craft that is not the controlled one is applied by that craft's own guidance step (`GuidanceWindow.RequestStop`).

RELEASE ALL, in the head drawer and in the AFC menu, keeps its call, `ResetFlightComputer`, and its engine policy: it ends every AFC guidance mode on the craft, and the engine is cut unless an abort already left it as it was. Stock AUTO comes back only together with that cut. It shows only while a guidance mode flies, guidance still holds the controls, or guidance state is left that a release frees (`GuidanceWindow.HoldsReleasableState`, the same test `ReleaseVehicle` makes, or the AutoStage arm guidance made). It also clears an entry guidance request that has not engaged yet, because the release would not stop the next step from taking it.

The console writes two guidance switches by itself, from the draw: when the mode that flies the landing changes, `GuidanceWindow.FollowLiveLanding` sets `UseThreeDofLanding` and `UseSixDofLanding` to that mode (entry guidance, touchdown guidance or G-FOLD), per craft, so the LAND page and its commit act on what flies. These switches choose a page and the braking burn hand-over of a later deorbit; they write no actuator.

The three per-craft switches are the GNC SWITCHES in the head drawer: AFC MAY STEER (`Engage`), AFC MAY STAGE AND THROTTLE (`AutoStage` on the guidance state) and AUTOSTAGE (`StagingDetector.Arm`, the same call the AUTOSTAGE gauge button and the AUTOSTAGE THIS CRAFT switch on STAGING make). While AFC MAY STEER or AFC MAY STAGE AND THROTTLE is off, a warning row in the head says so and opens the drawer. Neither switch locks while a mode flies. AFC MAY STEER off while a mode holds the craft is a hand-back (`ApplyAutopilot` calls `HandBackVehicle`): every mode ends and the engine is cut, also in the air. AFC MAY STAGE AND THROTTLE off gates only the engine and staging commands, so AFC keeps steering, except that a landing with a deorbit request or in its landing burn ends through `AbortLanding`. Touchdown guidance reads neither switch. While guidance armed AutoStage for the flight that runs, the AUTOSTAGE switches say so in their tooltip and still disarm it; guidance keeps that disarm for the rest of the flight.

## Identity and persistence

RCS and MultiPass registries use save and vehicle IDs.
`SharedVehicleHooks.OnRenamed` moves their entries, stored vehicle IDs, MultiPass burn-mode history and the claim's recorded ID.
Disposal removes the vehicle's registry state.
The execution registries persist to TOML; ownership claims remain runtime state.

## Verification limits

`afc-control-write-surface` compares public `FlightComputer` field values around one RCS activation and cancellation.
Its shallow snapshots do not observe mutations inside referenced objects, private inputs, properties, per-step outputs, staging, Guidance or writes that leave a value unchanged.
The field list is maintained separately from this document.
It is a regression check for that scenario, not an automatic audit of every write or release path.
